# The collector

Status: draft for the collector, October 16, 2026. Gallery voice, with the
collector's published words quoted and dated. For Jamie to correct and add
to; the page can carry more of his own writing if he wants it to.

---

This collection exists because one person kept buying. Jamie Thingelstad is
a technology executive in Minneapolis who has kept a public record of his
life and work for decades: a blog, a newsletter called The Weekly Thing, and
a habit of writing down what he is enjoying. In March 2022 he wrote that he
had found a drawing board called Nifty Ink through a grant listing and "loved
it as soon as I discovered it." The appeal, in his words, was that it was not
about money: "This isn't about buying NFTs and going to the moon. This is
about a super-simple low-barrier platform that allows anyone to create, sell,
buy created images for very low costs. Many are a dollar. All about fun."

He made things on it before he collected much. In April 2022 he drew the word
UKRAINE in block letters, his "one talent," minted fifty copies at ten dollars
and matched every sale with a donation. On the first of May his daughter
Mazie drew her first ink, "Sunshine and Rainbows," two rabbits in a garden,
and sold a copy within hours. At the end of May he bought a hundred copies of
a stranger's ink that said HELLO and sent one to every reader who held a
newsletter token.

The first nnxyz drawing in the family was a gift, and it came before the
first purchase. On April 25, 2022, the artist minted "200 nifties," the
drawing made to mark two hundred inks on the board, straight into Jamie's
wallet. nnxyz gave the milestone pieces to the collectors who were paying
attention, and by late April Jamie was one of them. He bought his first nnxyz
inks on May 10, and the same day two smaller wallets, Tyler's and Mazie's,
each bought three. A day later he wrote: "I'm the top collector on Nifty Ink.
I just have so much fun here." On May 23 the artist minted New Nifty on the
Block #100 into all three family wallets, unpaid, and on June 20 did the same
with "300 nifties." The milestones of the artist's year went to the family as
they happened.

From there the collecting became a project of its own. Jamie bought nnxyz
inks all summer, and in August and October 2022 the artist delivered the
complete sets of Nifty Faces and New Nifty on the Block by direct transfer,
hundreds of drawings at a time, together with the origin works, the ones
titled #0000 that the artist held back as the key to each series. In
September Jamie drew "nnxyz fan art #0001," his block letters spelling the
artist's name over a scribbled ground. On Thanksgiving morning he explained
chains and bridges to two friends over brunch by moving an nnxyz ink from
Gnosis to Ethereum. In January 2023, when the artist came back to the board
after two months away, he wrote "I was excited to see nnxyz, one of my
favorite artists, publishing again," and bought Rawblocks #49 "to
celebrate." In March 2023, with the series ending, the artist sent the last
batch, and Jamie moved his 736 inks into a wallet named for the board. In May
he bought Abstract #0000 and with it the fifty-two paintings, "the fourth
nnxyz collection that I have now."

He built a gallery for them on his own site, five pages of images with the
dates and the transactions, and kept it there for three years. This site
replaces it. His statement on the front page says why: "I always intended to
share these collections. I wanted to give them a proper home where the art
could be experienced fully, including the rare ability to watch each image
being drawn, stroke by stroke." The drawings earned their maker about four
thousand dollars in three years. About half of that came from the wallets on
this page. The ledger does not record enjoyment, but his blog does, and by
his own account there was a lot of it.
