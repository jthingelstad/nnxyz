# What the collection says: a thesis for the rooms

Status: Phase 1 draft, October 16, 2026, for the collector to mark up before
any room is rewritten. Written after looking at all 28 rooms as walls (the
contact sheets built from the published images), the on-chain record in
`curation/research/onchain/`, and the artist's few public words. Voice: the
gallery's, as amended on October 16. Readings are stated; where one rests on
the record or on the artist's words, the sentence says so.

What this document is for: every room rewrite inherits it. A room may argue
with it, but not ignore it. The wing paragraphs become the new wing
introductions, and the collection thesis feeds the project page and the
front-page welcome.

---

## The collection thesis

nnxyz drew one thing for three years: a face with its teeth showing. It is a
head in a square, eyes made of targets, spirals or an X, and a mouth drawn as
a grille of bars. Nearly six hundred times the artist sat down at a free
drawing board on a cheap chain and made that face again, and the face was
never the same twice. The collection is the record of what one mask can hold.

**The grin and the snarl are the same mouth.** The first thing the face does
is show its teeth, and the teeth do not say whether the face is laughing or
about to bite. That is not a failure of drawing; it is the subject. A row of
white bars in a red box reads as a grin on a yellow ground and as a snarl on
black, and the artist uses both readings, sometimes in the same week. The
collector's first response to the work, joy and anger side by side, is
accurate because the drawings are built to hold both at once. The gallery can
say so, and it can say which works tip which way: the box heads of October
2021 grin; the scribbled heads of the summer of 2022 grind their teeth; the
skulls in the dark bare them.

**The face is a mask, and now and then the mask comes off.** Most of the
faces are apparatus: boxes inside boxes, grilles, zigzags, a crown. But in
every series there are a few faces that are simply people. The pale portraits
of early 2022, drawn in soft loops, look back with tired eyes. The Rawblocks
portraits of November 2022, a man in a plaid shirt with a cigarette, a man
hunched in a cap, a woman in yellow light, are people who have had a long
day. They are the quietest works in the collection and they are where the
collection is most tender. The rooms should treat them as the face behind the
mask, because that is what they look like, hung among the grilles.

**The drawings belong to a place and a year.** The place was Nifty Ink, a
drawing board built by Austin Griffith on the xDai chain, where anyone could
draw and mint for a few cents; the record shows Griffith funding the artist's
first Ethereum wallet and buying eighteen inks. The year was 2022, a boom that
became a bust. The drawings do not illustrate the market, but they speak its
language. The titles and the words scrawled inside the pictures are the
scene's slang: "cash lightning", "STILL BULLISH @NIFTYINK", "WAGMI", "GM",
"pizza day", a cauldron labelled "SCAFFOLD ETH", and, in two of the Other
works, a satire written across the picture: "memewashed artfluence",
"sold by Christie's", "blue chip OG". The milestone pieces, "100 nifties",
"200 nifties", "300", "500 inks", are the artist counting their own output
in public. The record adds what the pictures imply: the artist liked 345
other people's inks, gave paintings to the people who bought, and spent the
year inside a small circle that is named on the project page. The gallery may
say the work is of that scene, in that year, because the work says it.

**The mood of the year is in the rooms.** Read in date order, which is how
every room is hung, the collection has weather. The winter of 2021 to 2022
is bright and confident: box heads, crowds of grinning figures, skeletons
dancing on flat color. May 2022 is the month of beams, when eyes become
lamps and a searchlight crosses every picture; it is also the month the
collector's family began buying and the month the market first broke. The
summer of 2022 is the mask summer, ten weeks in which almost every drawing
is a head buried in scribble on a dark ground, teeth showing. Late August
brings cats and then, for the first time at scale, portraits of people with
worried eyes. Rawblocks begins in late October 2022 and its first week of
portraits, November 5 to 9, is the week a large exchange collapsed; the
pictures are of men smoking and sitting. The series ends in March 2023 with
two landscapes and no face in either. The gallery does not need to name the
exchange. It can say that the faces grow quieter as the year goes on, that
the crowd of grins in December becomes a crowd of X eyes by June, and that the
last two pictures leave the room.

**Closing was part of the work.** The record is explicit. Each series ends on
an origin work, #0000, held back and sold last as the key to the complete set.
Every unsold edition slot was minted to a dead address, 2,895 of them, 1,560
in March 2023 alone, so that nothing more could be bought and each numbered
series was finished. The Abstract copies went the same way in July 2023. Then,
on Ethereum, the artist made "i was famous", Duchamp's urinal four times over
in Pop colors, and "lord of the inks", a single image of about five hundred of
their own drawings laid out as a wall. The gallery reads these as the artist
looking back at the project and naming it: a performance, a crowd of masks, a
short fame. The titles say it; the burns and the wall confirm it.

**The artist has a theory of the work, and it fits.** The depth pass of
October 16 (`curation/research/social/`) recovered the artist's bios and about
2,700 posts. The bios, in full: "born readymade to please the eye / flawed
imperfect on purpose" (Linktree); "born readymade to please the eye,
intentionally flawed to show the arbitrariness of insignificance" (objkt); "A
drama of the arbitrariness & insignificance of latent realities in the visible
public space" (Instagram and Threads). The posts add the rest. "Art is an
imprint of the time and society in which an artist moves. Art is therefore
always ethical and political, even if an artist denies it" (July 30, 2025).
"There is no such thing as bad or good art, there are only reasons for creating
it, the meaning that a work may have as a result and the form it follows" (June
20, 2024). "It is not the task of a work to immediately open itself fully to
the recipient, nor is it the task of the artist to explain his tools or his
process" (June 3, 2024). And on feeling: "wanting to convert every bad feeling
into a good one is an absolutely unhealthy disorder in our society" (June 18,
2024); anger "is a completely normal feeling for a human being" (June 10,
2024).

The gallery takes the artist at their word, and the words license what the
rooms want to do. The drawings are made to please the eye and are flawed on
purpose; the grin that is also a snarl is that flaw, built in. The work is an
imprint of its time and place, so reading the year's weather in the rooms is
reading the work the way its maker says work should be read. The artist will
not explain the pictures, so the rooms must, and may. Everything the rooms say
should come back to that pair: a face made to please, and a mouth that will not
say whether it is pleased.

**The closing was announced, and named.** "All 400 created nifties will be
gradually burned. Everything after that is part of a new story" (August 23,
2022). "My first ink will be the last burned" (August 11, 2022). "I'm extremely
bullish about Nifty Ink and my nifties; I won't stop until the site shuts down"
(April 8, 2022). The index collection was named after Warhol's Index Book,
"speaking of unloved works being lost" (August 2, 2022), and the artist's
advice to collectors the day before: "collect and curate more art that you
don't like, because the artist who isn't what you're looking for today may have
evolved into someone tomorrow". The Abstract gifts were "a small thank you"
to everyone who had bought on the board (April 12, 2022). The satire written
into the Other works ("sold by Christie's", "memewashed artfluence") has a
sequel in the artist's own words two years later: a series about "sick systems
that value work only on the basis of vanity metrics", and "just swap me out for
your favorite artist and you won't notice any difference" (May 30, 2024).
Every quotation above is in `curation/research/social/x-posts.json` by id and
date; the project page carries them verbatim.

**What the gallery compares the work to, as its own comparison.** The crowns,
the skulls, the X eyes and the words written into the picture put the drawings
near Jean-Michel Basquiat, and "i was famous" puts the artist's own frame near
Duchamp and Warhol. The scribbled heads sit near art brut. These are the
gallery's comparisons. The artist has named Duchamp (by drawing the urinal, and
in a post of January 11, 2025: Duchamp and Dada "still presuppose intention,
everything else is just entertainment that would like to be art"), Warhol (the
Index Book), and documenta 5 (March 23, 2022). The artist has not named
Basquiat. The text says "near", never "influenced by", except where the artist
named the name.

## The wings

### Faces (134 works, October 2021 to July 2022)

The mask is invented here, and the wing should say what it is for. A box head
with a grille of teeth is drawn on the first day and is still the subject in
the collection piece, where the letters of the name fill the square: the face
and the signature become one thing. Along the way the wing holds the
collection's full range of feeling in miniature. The box heads grin. The faces
in the tangle scream or stare out alarmed. The skulls in the dark are fear
drawn as a face. The modelled heads of the spring shine like carved wood and
look back without expression. And the pale portraits of the winter are the
first faces that are simply people, tired and quiet. The wing's argument: the
mask was built to please and to bite, and in this first series the artist
finds out that it can also be taken off.

### New Nifty on the Block (303 works, December 2021 to October 2022)

The title is a joke about a boy band and a word for a neighborhood, and the
wing is a year in that neighborhood. Part one is the block as a crowd: figures
made of line, grinning ghosts standing shoulder to shoulder, black creatures
with X eyes radiating wavy lines on flat color, skeletons in hats. Hung among
them are a figure hanging upside down behind red bars, a figure in a cage of
rain, and in May the beams, when the eyes become lamps and a searchlight
picks out what the drawing wants you to see. Part two is the mask summer: ten
weeks of heads on dark grounds, buried in scribble, teeth grinding, with the
few drawings that were not masks, a dog, a tree, a valley, a man with a
balloon in a city, kept together as what the artist drew in between. Part
three turns to portraits, heads with brows and lids and worried eyes, and
closes on the three works of October 15, 2022: the first drawing redrawn, #300,
and the collection piece. The wing's argument: this is the collection's diary.
The crowd of grins in December becomes a crowd of X eyes by June and a row of
worried faces by September, and the mask gets heavier as the year goes on.
The gallery states the dates and lets the visitor feel the weather.

### Rawblocks (66 works, May 2022 and October 2022 to March 2023)

After the party. The series opens with a week of portraits painted fast,
November 5 to 9, 2022: men smoking, hunched, hooded, a woman in yellow light.
They are the collection's most human pictures, and they came at the moment
the scene around the artist was coming apart. The patterned masks and flat
black figures that follow are the old vocabulary used with more economy, and
the winter heads are dark and toothy again. Then the collection piece, and in
the two hours after it, two landscapes: sun through a stand of trees, light
falling in a forest onto fallen trunks. No face. The wing's argument: Rawblocks
is the series in which the mask is set down, picked up once more, and then the
artist walks out of the picture. The record agrees: the unsold editions of
everything were burned the same month.

### Abstract (52 works, March 30 to April 4, 2022)

A week of painting, on a different chain, with no faces at all: bands, bars
and washes in one room, splatter, scribble and swirl in the other. The record
makes the wing's argument for it. Forty-six of these paintings were given
away, in April 2022, to the people who had bought, liked and made work on the
board: Griffith, scrimshaw, jasperart, danielsheldon, superphiz and others
named on the project page. They are the gentlest things the artist made, and
they were gifts. The gallery can say that the calm is the point, and that it
was aimed at particular people.

### Other nnxyz (45 works, October 2021 to December 2024)

The occasions and the afterwards. One room is the artist in public: milestone
self-portraits at 100, 200, 300 and 500 inks; the summer figures in NIFTY INK
shirts with a bottle of champagne and the word WAGMI; pizza day; GM; and the two
drawings with the satire written on them. The other room is what came after the
series ended: numbered masks from 2023 and 2024, denser and heavier than
before, drawn at a rate of a few a month for an audience that had mostly gone.
The wing's argument: the artist drew the scene while it was a scene, and kept
drawing the face after the scene was over.

### Scrimshaw (25 works, mostly February 16, 2021)

A different artist, kept because the collector saw a kinship, and the wall
shows it is real. Scrimshaw's heads bare their teeth too: rows of white bars in
a red mouth, eyes as rings and an X, hair as a storm of line, a crowned skull
on black. They were drawn on the same board in February 2021, eight months
before nnxyz's first ink. The gallery can state the dates and the shared
vocabulary, and must stop there: who saw whom is not in the record. What the
record does hold is a thread. nnxyz liked three of scrimshaw's inks, and
scrimshaw received an Abstract painting in April 2022. The wing's argument: the
mask with teeth was already on the board when nnxyz arrived, in another hand,
and the two artists share a room in this collection because they share a
mouth.

### After Nifty Ink (proposed room; 38 owned works new to the site)

The depth pass found far more than six. Beyond the Optimism works ("Heaven or
Hell", an animated hooded face; "oink", a painted landscape; "opticals",
generative scatters), the collector holds on Polygon seven "nifty faces px",
the pixelated twins the artist airdropped to every Nifty Faces holder in April
2022; 22 "chic city cat club" cats ("no roadmap, no utility"); 14 "Friends Of
Country Air"; four "cringe-eez doodles"; one "pxlpop"; and "nnxyz" #5. Several
are held twice because the artist re-issued three contracts and airdropped the
copies to holders. With "i was famous" and "lord of the inks" as references.
The room's argument: when the artist left the board the face went with it,
first into pixels (the px twins are the Faces, literally, at lower resolution),
then into jokes about collections (cats with no roadmap), and what remained of
the hand was its weather (landscapes) and its marks (opticals), with the look
back at fame as the frame. How to hang the re-issued pairs is the collector's
call.

## What the stroke study should test

Claims the rooms would like to make and the measures that would confirm or
correct them (`tools/analyze-strokes.py`, output in
`curation/analysis/strokes/`):

- The ground is laid first in nearly every work, and the teeth come last.
  Expected: first-stroke coverage above a quarter of the canvas in most
  works; the whitest, narrowest strokes concentrated in the final deciles.
- The mask summer is drawn by burying: high overpainting and many buried
  strokes in June to August 2022 compared with the spring.
- The black silhouettes are the sparest works by far, and the collection
  pieces the densest.
- Rawblocks portraits use wide translucent strokes (paint) with a few thin
  opaque lines on top; the Faces box heads are the reverse.
- The late masks of 2023 and 2024 are heavier: wider median strokes, more
  overpainting, than the 2022 masks.
- Sessions: works cluster in evenings UTC and in runs of consecutive days;
  the record should show the drawing sprees the rooms describe (July 9, 2022,
  six masks in a day; November 5 to 9, 2022).

Where a measure contradicts a sentence above, the sentence changes.

## What the depth pass changed

- The artist does explain, at length, on X. The thesis now quotes the artist
  where the quotation carries weight, and the project page carries the rest.
- Nifty Ink's own statistics for the main address (read October 16, 2026): 595
  inks created, 281 sold, 442 likes, $4,040 earned. The on-chain count of 592
  successful createInk transactions is close; the page's 595 is the number to
  cite.
- Every nnxyz ink the collector holds is already in the archive (632 tokens,
  574 distinct inks). The December 2024 inks #64 and #66 came to the collector
  in April 2025; #65 went to another buyer.
- The After Nifty Ink room grows from six works to 38.

## Open questions for the collector

1. Does the thesis land for you? What is missing from what you felt when you
   first saw the work?
2. "The grin and the snarl are the same mouth" is the spine. Is it the right
   one?
3. The year's weather is stated with dates and without naming the market
   events. Say if you want the events named (the May 2022 and November 2022
   collapses are public record) or left implicit.
4. The Basquiat comparison will be obvious to many visitors. It is written as
   the gallery's. Keep it, or leave it to the visitor?
5. Pilot room: "Skulls and Shadows" (Faces, dark) would carry the fear reading;
   "Scribbled Heads" (NNOTB, summer) would carry the mask-summer reading. Which
   first?
