Faces Built from Boxes17 works · 5 chapters

How do rectangular eye sockets and mouth shapes organize a face?

Rectangular frames divide these faces into eye zones, brow bands and mouths. Follow those divisions through flat color areas and increasingly layered lines, then compare works whose compartments are less easy to separate. The rectangles remain useful landmarks even when curved and diagonal marks cross them. Which shapes make recognition possible?

The Basic Grammar

Three works establish the core structure: a face divided into hard-edged zones, each feature assigned its own rectangular compartment with a distinct interior.

Flat Grounds, Direct Address

Four works place the rectangular face against a single saturated color, removing spatial depth and pushing the assembled features into unmediated contact with the viewer.

Compartments at Capacity

Five works fill each rectangular zone with competing marks — crossed lines, spirals, packed strokes — until the interior of every compartment is working against its own boundary.

Scaffolding Made Visible

Four works pull back from maximum density toward a structural, diagrammatic quality: the face reads as a plan or diagram rather than a presence, and the scaffolding itself becomes the subject.

The Frame Under Strain

The final two works push the rectangular structure to its limit: marks escape their zones, labels appear on the grid, and the geometry reads as something it can no longer fully organize.

Return to an eye socket or mouth in an earlier work. Does its rectangular frame make the feature clearer, or draw attention to the marks crossing it?
