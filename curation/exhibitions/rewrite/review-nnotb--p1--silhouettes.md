# Black Silhouettes (nnotb--p1--silhouettes)

Spoken words: 1823, about 12.2 minutes.

## Introduction

For ten weeks in the spring of 2022 the same formula held: one flat color, one black shape, and a ring of short wavy lines. Twenty-eight works came out of it, from a snarling cat on March 25 to a cat the size of a hill on June 2. These are the friendliest drawings nnxyz made all year, and they were made in the busiest ten weeks of the artist's life on the board, when the inks were selling, the airdrops were going out, and more than seventy works went by in a single month.

Inside the black there is almost nothing. A pair of spiral eyes, or two small X marks, and sometimes a row of jagged teeth. A spiral makes a cat dizzy or wild. An X closes an eye for good, and the same X on a rabbit, a bee and a whole crowd of heads runs through the room like a signature. The wavy lines are the other constant. They sit in the color around the figure, never touching it, and they make a still black shape hum, as if every creature here were giving off heat.

The cats and the scarecrows had a second life. On May 1 the artist told collectors that whoever held certain inks by the middle of the month would receive new work on another chain, and in July the cats became the chic city cat club, a Polygon collection of nearly ten thousand cats, one sent free to every holder and the rest offered for a single MATIC. The whole room came to the collector at once: twenty-seven of these works were sent to Jamie Thingelstad's wallet on October 16, 2022, the day after the series ended. Watch the black horizon arrive under the creatures, watch the figures turn hairy, and watch, in the last days, the black become nearly all there is.

## Cats on a Ledge

The room begins with four cats. The first arrives in March, the other three in one week of April, and between them they set every rule the next ten weeks will follow.

**#49** (label) A black cat arches over a scribbled yellow and white ground, its back one long diagonal across the square. The eyes are two red spirals in orange boxes, and the mouth is a white zigzag of teeth. Thick blue wavy lines fill the teal around it. It is the first black silhouette in the collection, from March 25, 2022, and the only one that snarls. The artist posted it to X the same day with a link to the ink, the way nearly every work in this wing was announced: a title, a number, and the address on the board. Everything that follows in the room is this cat with the anger taken out.

**#51** (line) The same cat lies on a black ledge with its tail up, and nothing is drawn inside it but two yellow spirals and two white fangs.

**#52** (label) One day later the same cat lies on the same ledge, facing the other way. Its body is filled with yellow scribble, so the black shows only at the edges, and the cat turns from a cut-out into something lit from inside. The eyes are red targets with crosses. Set it beside the cat from the day before and the whole method of the room is visible: a black shape is a decision about what to leave out, and filling it back in makes a different animal.

**#53** (line) Balanced on a black ball, a cat curls its tail over its head, with blue spiral eyes and a white saw-tooth grin.

## Three Days in April

Six works in three days, April 25 to 27. The cat gives way to other creatures, a black horizon appears under them, and two are set inside a frame. On April 25 the artist also minted a drawing called 200 nifties, to mark two hundred inks on the board, straight into the collector's wallet, the first nnxyz work he was given.

**#58** (label) Two cats sit facing each other on a red ground, and between them a heart made of two black bars meets at its point. Both have yellow spiral eyes and a jagged yellow grin. Yellow lines fill the red in rings around them. Two cats and a heart is the gentlest picture in the series, and it was made on April 25, the day the artist celebrated two hundred inks on the board and sent the celebration piece to the collectors who had been paying attention. The affection in the drawing and the affection in the gesture are the same thing.

## Scarecrows and a Witch

After three weeks away, the black turns hairy. Scarecrows are drawn in bristling strokes, grass grows along the horizon, and the X mark arrives for the eyes. These are the inks the artist named in the May airdrop: hold a cat or a scarecrow, and something new would follow.

**#88** (line) On a single pole, a scarecrow holds its arms out, drawn in scratchy strokes, with grass bristling along the black horizon and every wavy line in black on orange.

**#89** (label) The scarecrow again, a day later, now standing in a field with a black cat beside a purple fence. Its face has two round yellow eyes and its body is a mass of hatched strokes. Yellow wavy marks are everywhere in the magenta. This is the drawing that gave the room's two formulas to each other: the hairy scarecrow and the flat cat share one field. The artist promised the scarecrow holders their own collection and, as late as July 31, was still telling them it had not been forgotten. The cats got their club; the scarecrows are still waiting at the fence.

**#91** (line) A rabbit sits upright on red with both ears straight up, its eyes two X marks and its mouth a small zigzag, a line down its chest like a seam.

**#92** (line) Dancing on one leg, a round-headed figure bends its arms, X eyes, the wavy lines black again on orange.

**#96** (label) A witch in a pointed hat stirs a cauldron from a step ladder, and the cauldron says SCAFFOLD ETH in scratchy pale letters. The witch is drawn like the scarecrows, in bristling strokes with one X for an eye, and sparks rise from the pot. Scaffold-ETH is the open-source toolkit Nifty Ink was built with, made by Austin Griffith, the engineer who built the board. He was one of the artist's earliest buyers, and in February 2022 he put ether into the artist's Ethereum wallet so the inks could be upgraded to the main chain. This is the one drawing in the room about where the room came from: a witch cooking the thing that made her possible, and enjoying it.

## Small Creatures

The last week of May brings the small things: a snail, a turtle, a bee, a balloon, a worm, a spider, a flower and a crowd. Each gets one shape, one color and its ring of lines. Eight of them were drawn on two days, May 25 and 31.

**#101** (line) Along the horizon crawls a snail with a yellow spiral shell and a tiny cat-like face.

**#106** (line) Over the horizon a bee with X eyes hovers, while a small black flower with spiral eyes looks up at it from the lower right.

**#109** (line) On a long wavy string a balloon with spiral eyes and a jagged grin rises, and a black hand at the bottom corner lets it go.

**#112** (line) Out of a bitten apple on a steep black slope rises a worm with X eyes, the lines around it black on green.

**#114** (line) From the top edge a spider hangs on a thin thread, legs tucked around a round body, three small yellow marks for a face.

**#119** (label) A crowd of black heads fills the bottom of a yellow square, every face a pair of X marks. The yellow sits inside a thick black border, so the square is a window and the crowd stands at its sill. Not one of them has a mouth. It is the only crowd in the room, and it comes at the end of May, when the market had just fallen and the board was full of people wondering what they were holding. The X eyes are the room's joke about being closed for good, and here there are twelve of them, all looking out of the same window, and nothing to see.

## Last Days

The room ends on June 1 and 2 with four works that take the formula in four directions: a figure drawn in scribble, a child reaching up, a page of tiny figures with no color at all, and one last enormous cat.

**#126** (line) Bent over two crates marked with sunbursts, a scribbled figure rests its hands on the lids, while a small snail crosses the pink at the bottom left.

**#130** (label) A figure seen from behind reaches up to a small black heart that gives off sparks. The face is turned away, and all that shows of it is one small X on the cheek. The wavy lines, black now, stand up along the arm and the head like hair on end. A child reaching for a spark is the most tender picture in the wing, and it was made on June 1, 2022, in the week the first part of the series ended. The heart is tiny and the yellow is enormous. Everything in the drawing is about the distance between the hand and the thing it wants.

**#132** (line) Rows of tiny black figures tumble across a white page, each one scribbled in a few strokes, with a single red one beside the signature.

**#133** (label) A cat the size of a hill crouches on a black slope, filling the square from edge to edge, and looks out with yellow spiral eyes and a stitched grin. Its tail rises off the top of the picture and comes back down. The blue outline is the same as the first cat's, the spirals are the same, and nothing else is. The first cat arched and snarled at the edge of a scribbled field; this one has grown until the field is gone. It is the last silhouette, from June 2, and the calmest. Ten weeks of black shapes end with the biggest of them, at rest.

## Closing

Go back to the first cat and the last. The one from March arches and bares its teeth on a scribbled ground. The one from June fills the whole square and sits still, with the same blue outline and the same spiral eyes. Between them the room tried the formula on twenty-six other creatures and never once turned mean. In the rooms next door the eyes are lamps and the heads are skulls. Here, for ten weeks, the black shapes were simply glad to be on the block.
