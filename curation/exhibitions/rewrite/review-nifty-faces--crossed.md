# Crossed-Out Eyes (nifty-faces--crossed)

Spoken words: 1949, about 14.1 minutes.

## Introduction

Box heads with their eyes struck through. In this room the eye is an X, a red cross, a plus sign, or a letter, and the box head of the first room comes back with its eyes cancelled. Twenty-six works, from December 1, 2021 to March 18, 2022, and then the collection piece, #0000, made in July. The grin from the first room is still here, under every crossed eye, and that is the room's unease: a face that cannot see you and is still smiling.

The cross is drawn many ways. A red X over a ring. A plus sign in a white box. Two red bars. And in a handful of works the eyes spell the artist's name: an X in one box, a Y in the next, a Z in the third. The letters N, N, X, Y and Z appear as eyes, as a brow, and on #48 as the whole face. An X for an eye is the cartoonist's sign for dead, or drunk, or knocked out. The artist takes that sign and makes it a signature. The name is in the eyes, so the one thing the face cannot do is look.

Watch the mouth under the crossed eyes. It is almost always a grid of white block teeth in a box, and as the eyes get stranger the teeth stay the same. Blind and grinning, head after head, until the room ends on the one work that puts every mark in the series into a single square, with the name drawn large and no face at all.

## The First Crosses

December 1 to 6, 2021. Eight box heads, the first with green crosses in its eyes, the last with the artist's name written across the whole face. Six days, and the eye goes from target to cross to letter.

**#26** (label) A box head inside a white box frame on green, with two ringed eyes crossed by green plus signs, a V of red and white for a nose, and bands of yellow and red filling the face. Two small boxes with spirals sit at the sides like ears. It is the first crossed eye in the series, from December 1, 2021, drawn not as a cancellation but as a plus sign laid over a target. A plus is an addition, and the face is still fine: the teeth are in their box and the ears are on. The room starts with the cross as a decoration. It does not stay one.

**#28** (none) (title only)

**#39** (line) A rounded box head on dark teal in cyan and red scribble, two red crosses in blue eye boxes, and a mouth of white triangle teeth, the crosses gone red and the teeth gone sharp, four days in.

**#40** (line) A box head on white in magenta and yellow scribble, two orange crosses in white eye boxes, and white block teeth in a tilted box, the same head in daylight, and no less blind for it.

**#42** (line) A block head on blue stripes with two orange ringed eyes crossed in white, and a mouth open on two rows of white teeth, upper and lower, the widest grin in the chapter on the only face that has lost both eyes to white.

**#46** (line) A horned owl mask on blue with a yellow zigzag crown, a white X in one eye and a dot in the other, and a grille of white teeth in a red box, one eye out and one eye open, and the open one is wary.

**#48** (label) The artist's name as a face. The letters N and N sit at the top in white, X Y Z in a box below them, a plus sign in cyan beside them, and a cross in a ring at the right. Below, a row of white ovals for teeth and a second row of ovals under a yellow wave. On purple and blue with yellow bars. Nothing here is a nose or a cheek; the face is made entirely of the name and its marks. Six days into the room, the artist has already drawn the conclusion: if the eyes are letters, the face is a signature. The teeth stay, two rows of them, because a signature in this collection still has to grin.

**#50** (label) A dark square with neon slashes of lime, red, pink and blue across it, a pink X in one eye and a heart in the other, and a brown drip running down the center of the face. The mouth is a pink bar at the bottom. Where the name was drawn as a face a few hours earlier, here the marks have been thrown at the square: the X is a slash, the slashes are the face, and the drip runs through all of it. One eye is crossed and the other is a heart, and the drip between them is the only thing in the room that looks like it hurt. The angriest picture in the Faces, and it was made the same evening as the signature.

## Crosses and Plus Signs

December 31 to February 8. Six heads over five weeks, with red crosses, plus signs and target eyes, and long fangs. The cross settles in, and the grins get longer in the tooth.

**#51** (line) A box head with coils at the sides like springs, two triangle eyes with red crosses, a big grid of white teeth, drawn on the last day of 2021, wound up and grinning into the new year.

**#52** (label) A box head on grey with two pink boxes for eyes, each with a white plus sign, a row of big white block teeth in a red box, and a comb of yellow and black spikes along the top for a crown. Drawn on the morning of January 1, 2022, in ninety-four strokes. A month after the first green crosses, the plus sign has become the whole eye: a white cross in a pink box, nothing else. New Year's morning, a crown, a grin, and no eyes at all. It is the room's cheerfulness at its most unsettling, and the artist drew it first thing.

**#57** (line) A head on yellow and white with a sunflower for one eye, green petals around a target, and a red cross for the other, two boxes of white teeth side by side, one eye in bloom and one eye out.

**#60** (none) (title only)

**#67** (line) A box head on dark brown with a red cross in one eye and a red X in the other, long white fangs in a dark mouth, and yellow wavy marks floating on the ground, the fangs the longest in the room, and the face still cannot see what it is baring them at.

**#70** (line) A box head on orange in pink and blue line, a red cross in one eye and a red X in the other, a grille of white teeth in a red box, and a crest of yellow strokes, bright, blind and cheerful.

## X, Y and Z

February 9 to 20, 2022. The eyes become letters. Six heads in which an X, a Y and a Z sit where the eyes should be. The face is now a word, and the word is the artist.

**#76** (label) A box head on lavender with three boxes in a row for eyes, and in them an X, a Y and a Z in red. Below, two boxes of white teeth side by side and a third box of teeth at the bottom. Horns of red line stand at the top corners. The name has moved from the brow to the eyes, one letter to a box, and the head has three eyes to hold it. Three eyes, three boxes of teeth, two horns: it is a machine for displaying a name, and the name is all it can see. The friendliest-looking of the letter heads, and the most completely blind.

**#78** (line) A box head on teal with a white X in one eye and a red cross in the other, a crown of yellow spikes, and a long bar of white teeth in a red box, crowned and cancelled.

**#80** (line) A round head on orange and purple with two star X eyes in boxes, a white nose, and a ring of white teeth around the chin like a collar, the teeth worn as jewellery now.

**#91** (line) A head on magenta with a yellow X in one eye and a blue target in the other, a yellow Y for a nose, a grin of white teeth and a second small mouth under the left eye, the name taking over the nose as well, and a second mouth growing where an eye should be.

**#92** (label) A box head on olive with three boxes for eyes in a row, X, Y and Z in red, a triangle for a nose like a house, a column of blue ovals, and a box of white teeth. Eleven days after the first three-eyed head, the letters are back in their boxes, and the head is built from blocks: a box for the eyes, a box for the nose, a box for the teeth. It is the tidiest head in the room, every part in a drawer, and the tidiness is the point: a face filed away under its own name.

**#96** (line) A box head with a yellow hat brim and red X eyes stands on a post like a sign, on orange: the face has become a signpost, and the sign says the name.

## Last Boxes and the Collection Piece

February 27 to March 18, 2022, five last box heads, and then the collection piece, drawn in July, which gathers every mark in the series into one square.

**#97** (line) A box head on purple standing on two posts, with two eyes of red bars in boxes, a yellow crest, and a long bar of white teeth, a face on stilts, out of reach and grinning down.

**#99** (line) A box head on yellow with goggles, two white ovals joined by a bar, each with a red cross, and a wide grin of white teeth, the goggles the last joke in the series: lenses for eyes that are crossed out behind them.

**#101** (none) (title only)

**#106** (none) (title only)

**#109** (line) A box head on maroon with a red and white X in a cyan box for one eye and a slash in the other, orange horns, a long nose, and a grin of white teeth, the last of the crossed boxes, from March 18, 2022, horned and blind and still pleased.

**#0000** (label) The collection piece, an edition of one, drawn on July 20, 2022, four months after the last numbered face. A dense square of pattern: the letters N, N, X, Y and Z drawn large in the center, a red heart at the top, a flower at the right, beads, zigzags, grids and teeth filling every corner. With 753 recorded strokes it is the most worked drawing in the Faces. The name that was a face in December is here the whole picture, and there is no face left around it: no eyes to cross, no mouth to grin, only the heart above the letters and the rows of teeth at the bottom edge, where they have become a border. The record says this work was held back and sold last, as the key to the complete set. The picture says the same thing in its own way. The series is over, and what remains is the signature, the heart, and the teeth.

## Closing

The first crossed eyes were green plus signs on a box head in December, and the head was grinning. The last work is the collection piece, where the letters of the name fill the square and there is no mouth left to grin with. The X started as a mark on an eye. By July it was the subject, and the subject was the artist, signing off on a series that could no longer see.
