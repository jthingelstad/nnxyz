# Line Figures and Crowds (nnotb--p1--line)

Spoken words: 1868, about 12.5 minutes.

## Introduction

Everything in this room is made of line. Figures are outlines, crowds are tangles, and where a body has color it is scribbled in, not painted. Twenty-three works, beginning with the first drawing in the whole series on December 8, 2021, and ending with four from the start of June. The series' name is a joke about a boy band and a word for a neighborhood, and the first week is the neighborhood as a crowd: more heads than you can count, pressed into one square.

The lines change their job as the months go by. In the first week they pile up until a square is full: a crowd of toothy heads, a figure with wings, two black bodies edged in neon. In January they thin out, and a whole scene can be a few white strokes on purple. By June a figure is one black shape with a white edge, standing alone in a grey room. The crowd of December is a crowd of grins. The figures of June are alone, and one of them holds a bird.

From the start the series was given away as much as sold. On December 14 the artist posted the tenth work and said it had been dropped straight into the wallets of the people who had bought the earlier ones. The first five works in the series went to Jamie Thingelstad on August 14, 2022, hours after he bought the origin piece of the Faces, and three of those five hang here. Then on October 16, the day after the series ended, the artist sent him the rest. Count the faces in each work as you go. The room begins with more than you can count and ends with one figure and one bird.

## The First Week

Six works from December 8 to 12, 2021, including the very first New Nifty on the Block. The square is packed every time. Three of these were among the five the artist sent the collector in August 2022, before the rest of the series followed.

**#1** (label) The first work in the series: a crowd of heads and raised limbs on cyan, drawn in red, blue, white and yellow scribble over black outline. Teeth are everywhere, in a skull at the top, a grinning head at the left, a jaw at the bottom edge. A hand with spread fingers reaches up at the upper left. Nothing is in front of anything else; the figures share one weave, and a face found in it is lost again as soon as the eye moves. It was drawn on December 8, 2021, seven weeks after the artist's first ink, and it announces a neighborhood: everyone on the block at once, all teeth, all waving. On October 15, 2022, the last day of the series, the artist drew it again, and that version hangs in the last room of the wing.

**#2** (line) A green-faced figure with X eyes sits cross-legged in an orange body with two yellow wings spread behind it, on a ground of white, magenta and black strokes.

**#5** (label) Two figures lean together on a pink and orange ground, one with yellow hair, both with round eyes and red mouths, and between them a large red heart. Two smaller hearts float at the sides. The bodies are drawn in green, yellow and white scribble with a thin white edge, and the big heart is outlined in white like a badge. It is the first drawing in the series with a heart in it, and with 378 recorded strokes the most worked in this room. Two people and a heart between them is as plain as a picture gets, and the artist drew it in the first week, before the masks, before the crash, when the block was new and everyone on it was a friend.

**#6** (line) Two black figures edged in white and pink stand against a dark ground of colored scribble, one with an X in its eye and the other with a Y, a hand of three pink fingers between them. The collector bought it on October 14, 2022, two days before the whole series arrived.

**#7** (line) One big face: two round red eyes with green X marks, a blue square eye between them, a mouth of white teeth, and thick swirls of red, blue and yellow line around the edge.

**#8** (none) (title only)

## Rows and Crowds

Mid December. The figures line up. Seven robed figures in a row, three lumpy ones side by side, a row of ghosts over black grass. The second of them, number 10, is the one the artist announced as a pocket drop for earlier buyers.

**#10** (line) A purple mass with four faces in it, each with a grin of white teeth and small yellow eyes, drawn in white line over an olive grid; the artist dropped it free into the wallets of the people who had bought the earlier works, and said so.

**#12** (label) Seven tall figures stand in a row, their bodies long stripes of blue, green, pink and yellow, their round heads burning with orange and red scribble. Each face has two small eyes and a grin. The heads touch a band of fire along the top, so the row seems to stand under one burning roof. It is the first row of figures in the series, and the tallest. Seven people in a line with their hair on fire, grinning: it is a band, or a lineup, or a block of neighbors standing outside a house that is burning, and pleased about it. In December 2021 that was what the market felt like from inside.

**#13** (line) Three lumpy figures in orange and blue scribble stand side by side on black, each with yellow eyes and a wide grin of white teeth.

**#14** (label) Six ghosts hang in a row under a pink sky, their long bodies drawn in thin blue, yellow and white lines that fall like rain, their faces screaming. Black grass stands up along the bottom in white outline. Three days after the row of seven burning heads, the row is back, but the fire has gone out: the heads are pale ovals with dark eyes and open mouths, and the bodies are see-through. Set the two rows together and the series has already found its two moods in its second week. The same lineup, grinning on Thursday and screaming on Sunday.

## Tangles

January and February 2022. The lines loosen and multiply, and the figures are harder to find. One hangs upside down behind bars.

**#19** (line) A tangle of blue, orange and black line over olive and red, with two grinning heads at the top corners and a white line running around the edge like a frame.

**#22** (label) A group of figures drawn in white, green and orange line on purple, their arms raised. One at the center has a dark face and both hands up. Twenty-nine recorded strokes make the whole drawing, and nothing is filled in; the purple shows through every body. It is the sparest crowd in the room, and the first where the line is nearly all that there is. Hands up can be a cheer or a surrender. On January 20, 2022, with the market sliding, the drawing does not say which.

**#23** (none) (title only)

**#25** (line) A figure hangs upside down behind five dark red bars on a red ground, its skull at the bottom with teeth showing, its legs crossed at the top, the first prisoner in the series.

**#35** (line) A figure in blue, orange and green scribble sits in a chair at a window, on pink, with a second small figure standing on the sill at the upper left.

## Spring Characters

One week in March. Four drawings that are each a scene or a character, with the line now doing the describing.

**#42** (line) A cartoon scene on teal and pink: three smiling faces in a box of yellow dots, a round green tree with a face, a yellow snail with antennae, and pink clouds along the top.

**#43** (line) A group of figures in white and yellow line on orange, a grinning monkey at the left and four others with round heads, over a ground of colored bars.

**#45** (line) A figure with square glasses and a wide grin crosses its arms, drawn in black, white and green hatching over an orange ground, spikes of hair standing up.

**#46** (line) A figure with a huge round head dances with one arm up and one out, drawn in red, white and blue line on dark teal, its face a swirl with a small grin.

## Four from Early Summer

After nine weeks, four works from May 30 to June 3. A crowd of yellow characters in Nifty Ink shirts, then two figures alone in grey rooms, then a pair with red heads.

**#117** (line) A crowd of yellow characters with spiky hair on pale blue, the one in front wearing sunglasses and a white tee that says NIFTY INK, pale faces floating behind them: the block in its uniform, two weeks after the crash.

**#121** (label) A black figure in a suit stands in a grey room, drawn with a thin white edge, its head stretched sideways like a balloon with two X eyes on it. A single yellow flower grows at the lower right. Thirty recorded strokes. In December the black figures were edged in neon and surrounded by scribble; here the same black body stands in nothing at all, and the room is empty except for the flower. It was drawn on May 31, 2022, at the end of the month the market fell. The suit, the grey walls and the cancelled eyes say what kind of month it was. The flower says the drawing is not finished with hope.

**#129** (label) A tall black figure with a white edge stands at the right of a grey room and holds out its arm, and on its hand sits a small purple bird with a yellow beak. The figure's face has two spiral eyes and a row of teeth. A darker square is drawn on the wall behind it. The bird is the one colored thing in the picture, and the whole arm is drawn to carry it. The day before, the figure in the suit stood in the same grey room with a flower it did not touch. This one has reached out and something has landed. Of all the works in the first half of the series, it is the one that most plainly says what the artist wanted from the block: not the crowd, but one small thing, alive, that comes to the hand.

**#136** (line) Two figures with red heads and black hair stand on a yellow ground ruled with white lines, a red heart between them and X marks on their faces, six months after the hearts of December, and the heart is broken down the middle.

## Closing

Go back to the first work and then to the figure with the bird. Both are made of line. One has more faces than you can count and fills the square to the corners; the other has one face, one arm and one small bird, and most of the square is empty wall. In December the artist was drawing a crowd to join. By June the crowd had thinned to one, and what it had left was something small and alive to hold out. The room is the distance between them.
