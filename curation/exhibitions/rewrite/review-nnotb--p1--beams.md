# Beams and Glowing Eyes (nnotb--p1--beams)

Spoken words: 1641, about 10.9 minutes.

## Introduction

In this room the eyes are lamps. Twenty-one works from May 2022 share one device: a head or a creature whose eyes give off light, and a beam that crosses the square like a searchlight. Where the light lands, the drawing goes pale and the lines show through it. Where it does not, the ground stays dense, scribbled in olive, red and blue. Twelve of them came in five days, May 7 to 11, and those were the five days the crypto market broke. Terra's stablecoin lost its peg on May 9 and its coin went to nothing by the 12th; the artist drew a head with its eyes blazing every day of it.

The light takes two forms. In the framed heads it is a hard wedge, drawn as a solid band of pale yellow laid over everything, with a bright white disc where it starts. In the painted creatures, the bears and the hooded figure, it is a halo, soft at the edges, with round spots of glare floating in it like dust in a projector. Both kinds do the same work: they fix where you look and leave the rest of the head in shadow. A searchlight is a nervous thing. It belongs to someone who needs to see.

The words say what the eyes mean. One work is titled cash lightning, and the artist gave it away to the three buyers who had just carried the inks past fifteen hundred xDai in sales. A teddy bear has BULLISH on its chest; a wolf two days later says STILL BULLISH, and that one, number 100, was minted free into sixty-two wallets, the collector's among them. On May 12, with the market on the floor, the artist posted that it was the best time to upgrade the inks to the main chain, and the glowing eyes in this room are that sentence drawn: bright, fixed, and not entirely sure.

## Five Days in May

Six framed heads open the room, drawn between May 7 and 9. Each one is packed to the frame with marks, and each one is cut by a beam. Over the same weekend the artist passed fifteen hundred xDai in sales on the board and said thank you with the fourth work here.

**#73** (label) A head tips to the right inside a tan frame, two square eyes glowing white, and a beam spills out of them toward the lower right corner. The mouth is a panel of upright teeth, lit from the same source. Around the head the ground is a weave of olive, yellow and blue scribble, with orange fence posts along the bottom. The light is drawn as a pale band laid over all of it, so the scribble shows through the beam like a pattern through gauze. It is the first lit head, from May 7, a Saturday. The beam does not come from anywhere; it comes from the eyes and goes out into the room, looking for something.

**#74** (line) Turned on its side, a machine of a head: a bar of four round dials, a stack of triangles, a grid for a cheek, and beams crossing the whole thing on the diagonal.

**#75** (line) One huge round eye on the left throws a wide beam down and to the right, past a small square eye and a grinning row of teeth.

**#76 cash lightning** (label) The same head again, under green light. The drawing is the one from #75 with the colors shifted: the beam is lime, the eye has a spiral in it, and the whole ground has gone cool. Its title is the only one in the room with words, cash lightning, and it was never for sale. On the evening of May 8 the artist thanked three collectors by name for helping to break the fifteen-hundred-xDai limit on the board and sent them this as an airdrop. Set the two heads side by side and the difference is the light alone, warm on one and acid on the other, falling on identical lines. The acid one is the thank-you note.

**#77** (none) (title only)

**#78** (line) Under a glass dome on a round base, a small creature with two glowing eyes sits in a flood of yellow rays, the whole picture lit from behind it.

## Bears and Helmets

Two days, May 9 to 11, and six works. A furry creature appears twice, once with its eyes lit and once in the dark, and then the heads put on helmets. These are the days Terra fell.

**#79** (line) A round, furry creature with two enormous glowing eyes squats on a patch of grass, orange stripes behind its head, its paws folded over its belly.

**#80** (label) The same creature, half an hour later, with the lights off. It sits in plain black now, inside a white border, and its eyes are two dark spirals where the lamps were. The fur is the same hatching, the paws are the same, the belly has the same pale patch. Without the glow the creature looks smaller and more worried, and the drawing is mostly shadow. May 9, 2022, was the day the stablecoin began to slip. The artist drew a creature with its eyes burning and then, before the hour was out, the same creature in the dark. Nobody has to decide which one is the portrait of that day. The room keeps both.

**#82** (line) In profile, facing left, a head sends two beams out of two square eyes, while a black band on the right edge holds the picture like a shoulder.

**#83** (line) A creature with a yellow beak and a row of hanging teeth like icicles, its eyes two glowing leaf shapes, set in a frame of pale blue.

**#84** (line) A blue head with a crest like a helmet, a wide grin of white teeth, and a single beam from a square eye at the upper left.

**#85** (line) Painted, not scribbled: a helmeted figure in gold and black holds its hands together under a grinning mouth, lit by a wide beam from the upper left, on May 11, with the market still falling.

## Eyes Like Lamps

After nine days away the glow comes back bigger. From May 20 to 22 the eyes become whole lamps, and two of the creatures start to speak. On May 20 the collector bought the first of them, the head with wild hair, for fifteen xDai.

**#86** (line) A head with a mop of wild yellow and black hair, a thin neck, and two orbs of light for eyes, each one larger than the mouth beneath it. Jamie Thingelstad bought it the day it was drawn, for fifteen xDai, his third purchase from the series.

**#90** (line) A teddy bear with sparking antennae sits in a burst of orange rays, its eyes two small lamps and the word BULLISH written across its chest, ten days after the crash.

**#93** (line) A head in white scribble over pink and orange, two beams driving to the right out of the eyes, three orange drips hanging from the top.

**#94** (none) (title only)

**#95** (line) An armored figure in black and gold, with a visor and one red eye, points a finger under a sky of blue rays.

## Two More Bears and a Skull

The room closes with the largest drawings in it: a hooded figure holding lights, a bear with glowing goggles, a wolf with a slogan that went out to sixty-two wallets, and a skull mask lit from inside.

**#97** (line) Inside a dark frame a hooded, bearded figure with glowing eyes holds out a hand, and round spots of light float around it like bubbles.

**#99** (label) A bear sits on a white ground, painted in soft grey and gold, its eyes hidden behind red goggles that glow. Two beams leave the goggles toward the lower left. On its shoulder, almost too small to see, stands a tiny figure. The bear is drawn with long, careful painted strokes, its paws resting on its knees, and the white around it is cloudy, not flat. At 817 recorded strokes, no other work in the room took as much drawing. It is the gentlest of the lit creatures: a bear that has sat down, and whose light is a pair of goggles it could take off. The tiny figure on its shoulder is the first passenger in the series.

**#100** (line) A wolf-like creature with tall ears and glowing eyes bares its teeth in the dark, beside the words STILL BULLISH in orange. Number 100 in the series, and the artist gave it away: on May 23 it was minted free into the wallets of the board's builder, Austin Griffith, two other collectors named in the post, and fifty-nine more people who held the inks, among them Jamie Thingelstad. The wolf is a joke with teeth. Two weeks after the crash, the artist's answer to the question everyone on the board was asking was a snarl and the word STILL, sent to everyone who had stayed.

**#113** (label) A skull mask fills a brown frame, its two eyes glowing yellow like headlamps, a beam falling from them to the lower right. Blue tubes rise from the top of the head, a crown of red zigzag runs under the cheekbone, and a necklace of white beads hangs across the jaw. The ground is a curtain of brown scribble. It is the last of the framed heads, and the one where the eyes and the beam are the same yellow as the paint. A skull with its lights on is the whole wing in one picture: the thing is dead and it is still looking.

## Closing

Go back to the bear with its lights on, and then to the bear with its lights off. Everything in the drawing is the same except the eyes, and the whole mood of the creature changes with them. Lit, it is a lamp in the dark; dark, it is small and worried. The artist drew both on the same day, half an hour apart, in the week the market fell. That is what the room is about: a light somebody decided to keep on.
