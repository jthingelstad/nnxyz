# Scribbled Heads (nnotb--p2--scribbled)

Spoken words: 1745, about 11.6 minutes.

## Introduction

Heads buried in line. In this room the face is drawn, and then drawn over, until it sits inside a tangle of colored scribble like a thing seen through weeds. Most of the grounds are black, slate or brown, and nearly every mouth shows its teeth. Twenty-eight works, from June 16 to August 19, 2022, and they are the darkest of the mask summer: the square and round masks next door are toys, and these are not.

Two things happen under the scribble. Some of the heads are painted first, with shadow on the cheek and light on the brow, and the line is laid over paint. Others are nothing but line, and the head is the place where the scribble gets densest. The difference is easy to see once you look for it: paint has edges that are soft, and line has none. In February the artist wrote that it is not the task of a work to fully reveal itself to the recipient, and called art a dope that makes every exploration unique. These heads are that sentence drawn: a face is there, and the line is what keeps it from being handed over.

The room's busiest stretch is one week, July 26 to 31, with fourteen heads in six days, in the middle of the month the artist was handing out cats to every holder and writing about an open-ended collection named after Warhol's Index Book. Watch the teeth across that week. They end up as the one clear thing in a face that has otherwise come apart, and then, on the last day of the summer, the scribble turns to soft gold rings and the face is only spirals.

## Painted Heads Under Line

June 16 to July 8. A profile on red, then a run of heads painted in gold and brown with the scribble drawn on top. The painted ones are the ones with feeling in them.

**#147** (line) A black skull in profile inside a black square on red, drawn in a few yellow and white lines, with a spiral eye, a row of yellow teeth and a stitched line running down the back of the head.

**#153** (label) A horned head painted in gold, brown and blue, with heavy lids and a bearded jaw, drawn over in pink, white and blue line. The paint underneath is modelled: the brow catches light, the cheeks fall into shadow, the beard is a block of dark strokes. The line on top loops around the horns and down the face without covering it. It is the first painted head in the room, made June 24, and the one where the paint and the line are most clearly two layers. The lids are heavy and the horns are small. This is a tired devil, and the scribble over it is not a disguise so much as weather.

## Mid July

Four heads from July 19 to 26. A red X returns to an eye, a brown head wears a bucket, and the line starts to take over. The cats were going out to every holder that week.

**#184** (line) A dark head with a red X in one eye, a yellow bar across the brow like a headband, and a grille of white teeth, drawn in dense colored scribble.

**#189** (label) A brown head on pale blue, painted in purple and tan with a flat top like a bucket, half-closed eyes and a grin of small teeth. Yellow and white dashes ring the head like a string of lights, and purple line loops around it. It is the one head in the room that smiles with its eyes closed, and the only one on a light ground. It was drawn on July 23, in the week the artist was sending a free cat to every holder of an ink and posting their names with thanks. A contented face, drawn once, in the middle of the summer's generosity, and then not again.

## Six Days in July

July 26 to 31, fourteen heads. The scribble is densest here, and the teeth are the one clear thing in most of them. In the same days the artist was naming the Index collection, a series that would only grow when every copy of the last piece had sold.

**#195** (label) A face in black scribble on orange, with a red X over one eye and a white ring for the other, a yellow bar across the brow, and two rows of white teeth in a dark mouth. The black line is drawn thickest here of any work in the room, in heavy vertical strokes down the cheeks. The X is as big as the eye socket and drawn in red over the black. It is the angriest head in the room, and it was made on July 26, the first of fourteen in six days. The cancelled eye, the bared teeth and the black pouring down the face say one thing together, and the orange behind them says it loudly.

## The Last Face

One work from August 19, made the same day as the last masks and a week into the burning: a face of spirals in soft gold.

**#231** (label) A face made of spirals: a square spiral for one eye, a round spiral for the other, a third spiral at the right edge like an ear, and a mouth of white zigzag. Everything is drawn in thin black and gold line over soft gold circles, and the whole square is a ring of rings. Where the first head in the room was a black skull in a box on red, this one has no edge at all. It is the last work of the mask summer, drawn August 19, 2022, with 57 strokes, a week after the artist began burning the unsold inks. Nothing in it is angry. The spirals go round and the gold is warm, and after ten weeks of teeth the summer ends on a face that is only dizzy, and at peace about it.

## Closing

The first skull is flat black in its box on red. The last face is spirals in gold. The one is all edge and the other is all circle. In between, the scribble closed over the faces and the teeth were what was left showing. A head that will not reveal itself is still a head that wants to be looked at, and the artist spent the summer drawing twenty-eight of them.
